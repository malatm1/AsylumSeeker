The receipt loop

From 10 November 2026, South Africa begins issuing asylum-registration receipts that shield first-time applicants from deportation while their claims are decided — the government's answer to the Constitutional Court ruling of 7 July. Supporters call it constitutional compliance. Critics call it a pull factor. Both are describing feedback loops.

This model lets you run them. It starts from the Department of Home Affairs' own October 2026 figures, then hands you the two levers that decide the next decade. Press play, or drag the levers mid-run and steer.

The two loops you are steering

R · reinforcing The pull loop

A receipt shields you from deportation. The risk of coming falls. Word spreads along the routes. More people register, more receipts circulate, and each turn amplifies the next — at word-of-mouth speed.

B · balancing The strain loop

More claims swell the backlog. Waits stretch into years. Legal and political pressure builds, capacity expands, and the backlog slowly drains — at government speed.

The whole policy question is which loop runs faster. Try the Status quo preset first: even with both levers at zero, 82 000 people stay stuck at nine-year waits for a decade, because the system was already running at the edge of its capacity before the receipts existed.

Grounded in the real numbers

  • ≈ 800 /month — new asylum applications. Fewer than 10 000 a year arrived every year from 2020 to 2025, apart from about 20 000 in 2023.
  • ≈ 780 /month — decisions. In the past year officials decided about 9 300 claims: 1 934 approved, 7 392 rejected.
  • 82 348 — active asylum cases form the starting backlog, alongside 161 000 appeal cases at the Refugee Appeals Authority, which sit outside this chart.
  • 223 000 — new applicants in 2009 alone. Arrivals have spiked more than tenfold before; that elasticity is what the pull lever turns.
  • − 19 000 — appeal cases cleared in 2025/26, the biggest reduction in years; that reform pace is what the capacity lever turns.

What this is. A conceptual system-dynamics model built for public reasoning, not a Department of Home Affairs forecast. The starting figures are real; every future on the chart is a scenario you chose. The arrivals response and reform pace are stylised from the historical record above.
